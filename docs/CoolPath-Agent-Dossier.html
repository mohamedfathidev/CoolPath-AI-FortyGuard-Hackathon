The problem

Heat is a mobility barrier — and it's getting worse.

Phoenix regularly spends more than half the day above 95°F in summer. For anyone on foot — commuters, students, delivery riders, people without a car — that heat isn't an inconvenience, it's a health risk that shapes whether a trip happens at all.

Conventional maps optimize a single thing: distance. They'll route a pedestrian down the shortest, most sun-exposed corridor without ever accounting for thermal load. But two routes of nearly identical length can differ meaningfully in how much extreme-heat exposure they carry — and that difference compounds as climate change pushes Phoenix summers hotter and longer.

The gap isn't data — FortyGuard already models street-level temperature. The gap is a tool that acts on that data: reads it, weighs it against distance, and hands a person a route they can actually walk in the heat. That's CoolPath.

The solution

Three ways in, one heat model underneath.

Every surface is powered by the same live FortyGuard temperature data and the same routing engine. What changes is how a person reaches it — a form, a sentence, or a city-wide map.

Technical · routing

Why exposure-hours, not degrees.

The single most important design decision in the product — and it came straight from the live data.

The obvious approach is to weight routes by the temperature at your departure time. We built that first. But live FortyGuard data showed that instantaneous temperature is nearly uniform — roughly a 0.5°F spread block-to-block across the pilot area. Everything downtown is uniformly hot at 2pm, so a route weighted on it is indistinguishable from plain shortest-path.

Cumulative daily heat exposure — how many hours a day a block spends above 95°F, from FortyGuard's exceedance analytic — tells a different story: a real spatial spread of 1–3 hours. That's the signal with texture, and it's arguably the more honest metric anyway (total thermal load, not one moment). So that's what drives the heat-optimized route.

Edge cost function

cost = distance_m × ( 1 + β × max(0, exposure_hrs − comfort_hrs) )

The routing itself is a hand-built A* with a binary min-heap over an OpenStreetMap walking graph, pulled per pilot city via Overpass and cached to disk. Each edge's cost is its length scaled by the heat exposure at its midpoint. The engine returns both the plain shortest path and the heat-weighted path, plus the concrete delta: extra distance, extra minutes, and hours of extreme-heat exposure avoided.

An honest limitation

On some date/route pairs the shortest and heat-optimized routes come out identical — when no genuinely cooler detour exists, that's correct data-driven behavior, not a bug. The demo defaults are chosen to show a real divergence.

Technical · the agent

A real tool-calling agent, not a scripted chatbot.

The agent decides for itself which FortyGuard endpoints to call and in what order, based on the user's sentence. Nothing is hardcoded.

A natural-language brief goes to the model with six tool definitions and a system prompt. The model emits function calls; the orchestrator executes them against the real pipeline, streams a live trace to the chat panel over Server-Sent Events, feeds results back, and loops until the model produces a final, source-cited answer (capped at 12 tool calls).

Resilient by design

  • Primary + fallback models. Google Gemini runs the agent; when its free-tier quota is exhausted, the turn automatically restarts on NVIDIA Nemotron. A 10-minute cooldown skips a known-exhausted quota instead of re-hitting it every request.
  • Clean output from a reasoning model. Nemotron's chain-of-thought is suppressed ("detailed thinking off") and stripped as a safety net, so answers stay concise instead of dumping raw reasoning.
  • Grounded and honest. Every heat claim traces back to a tool result and cites the FortyGuard capability that produced it; the real current date is injected so no model guesses it.
  • Key-safe. FortyGuard's signed heat-intelligence URL embeds the API key, so it's never surfaced to the model or browser — per FortyGuard's own guidance.
Technical · FortyGuard integration

Client-grade, by the rules.

The FortyGuard client is a single reusable module that honors the API's async contract, validation limits, and caching model — the "deployable, client-grade" bar, not a demo shortcut.

Async submit / poll

Every job is submit → activity_id → poll status with backoff, budgeted generously (up to ~3 min for wide areas, ~5 min for PDF reports). A timeout is distinguished from a genuine failure — only a failed task (which costs no credits) is safely resubmitted.

Validation & caching

U.S. coverage and the 2019→now+12h date window are enforced client-side before any call. Every result is keyed by rounded polygon, date, time, and analytic type and cached in Postgres — so an identical query never re-spends a credit and repeats are instant.

The climate lens

From a route tool to a heat-risk map.

The "Heat islands" feature surfaces the same exceedance data that quietly powers routing — turned outward into a city-wide climate view.

It fetches daily heat-exposure hours across a wide central-Phoenix polygon, colours every block on a cool-to-hot ramp, identifies and names the worst hotspots via reverse geocoding, and computes the heat-island gap — the difference between the most and least exposed blocks.

Auto-generated, data-grounded insight

"The hottest blocks endure 13.4 hours/day above 95°F — 3.0 more than the coolest, shaded areas. That gap is a textbook urban heat island: dense pavement and little tree cover trap heat long after greener blocks have relieved. As climate change pushes Phoenix summers hotter, these are exactly the blocks where cooling infrastructure delivers the most protection for people on foot."

It's the line that ties the product together: CoolPath doesn't just route around heat — it maps the climate risk and shows where intervention matters most.

Business · integration

A drop-in routing service, not just a UI.

The heat-aware routing is a plain REST endpoint any delivery, mobility, or logistics app can call. Send two points and a time; get the coolest viable route as JSON.

# POST /api/route
{
  "origin":      { "lat": 33.4749, "lon": -112.0892 },
  "destination": { "lat": 33.4515, "lon": -112.0703 },
  "date": "2025-07-20", "time": "14:00"
}

# → returns both routes + the trade-off
{
  "shortest":      { "distance_m": 4137, "duration_s": 2955, "avgExposureHours": 12.6 },
  "heatOptimized": { "distance_m": 4160, "coordinates": [[-112.0892, 33.4749], …] },
  "exposureReductionHours": 0.15
}

Each route ships a [lon, lat] polyline ready to drop onto a map, and exposureReductionHours quantifies the benefit in concrete terms. Roadmap: a versioned client SDK, API-key auth and rate limiting, and expansion to any FortyGuard-covered U.S. city.

Roadmap

Where it goes next.

  • Multi-city. The pilot is one Phoenix area done well; the architecture generalizes to any FortyGuard-covered U.S. city by pulling its OSM graph and re-pointing the bbox.
  • Published SDK & authed API. Turn the internal routing endpoint into a versioned @coolpath/client package with API keys and rate limiting — a real B2B service for third-party fleets.
  • Monitoring agent. Scheduled heat sweeps and alerts for a corridor or campus — deliberately out of scope for the one-day build, but a natural next feature.
  • Cooling-infrastructure planning. Layer tree-canopy and shade data onto the heat-island map to model interventions, not just observe the gap.
